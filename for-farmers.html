For Farmers

Rural communities deserve fair markets, strong infrastructure, and respect

Farmers are not asking for rhetoric. They need a government that understands market pressure, consolidation, land and water stewardship, energy reliability, and the reality that rural communities too often feel ignored until election season. Joshua Kent wants a policy agenda that treats rural strength as central to Michigan’s future.

What farmers and rural communities need

Joshua’s broader reform documents consistently connect rural strength to public resilience, infrastructure, and fair economic design.

Protection from concentrated power

Joshua’s platform is anti-consolidation by design. That matters in agriculture, where monopoly and oligopoly pressure can distort prices, weaken local choice, and leave farmers with less leverage over the conditions shaping their livelihood.

Land, soil, and watershed stewardship

Agriculture and ecology do not have to be enemies. Joshua wants policy that takes soil regeneration, watershed health, stormwater resilience, and practical environmental repair seriously so land can stay productive over the long term.

Infrastructure that actually reaches rural life

Rural communities need reliable energy, communications, transport, and water systems. Joshua’s infrastructure framework emphasizes durable, low-maintenance public assets and stronger resilience standards rather than waiting until systems fail.

What Joshua would fight for in Lansing

A Michigan House campaign cannot solve every national agricultural problem alone, but it can fight for fairer conditions and stronger public support.

Push back against anti-competitive concentration

Joshua wants a public economy that gives local producers, regional businesses, and community-rooted enterprises more room to survive and grow, instead of watching ever-larger players dominate supply chains, labor markets, and political access.

Strengthen rural resilience systems

His energy and infrastructure agenda supports microgrids for critical services, stronger utility reliability, better life-cycle planning, and systems that serve both rural and urban regions instead of leaving one side to absorb neglect.

Protect water and working land

Joshua’s policy papers call for stronger watershed protection, restoration where degradation is severe, and more serious stewardship of the natural systems agriculture depends on.

Keep opportunity from stopping at the county line

His education framework specifically emphasizes that rural students should not be locked out of advanced pathways, apprenticeships, certifications, or college-prep options simply because of geography.

The goal: rural and urban Michigan rising together

Joshua’s platform measures success in part by rural-urban cohesion, because a healthy state cannot be built on mutual neglect.

  • Rural Michigan should be treated as essential to public resilience, food systems, energy planning, and statewide prosperity.
  • Farmers deserve policy shaped around fairness, stewardship, and practical support rather than capture by concentrated power.
  • The campaign’s long-term direction is to build a government that sees local knowledge, public investment, and durable infrastructure as partners rather than opposites.

Bring rural voices into the campaign

Joshua Kent wants this agenda to keep growing through conversations with farmers, rural families, and communities across Michigan.
