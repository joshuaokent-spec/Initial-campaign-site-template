Research & Development

Invest in the knowledge, talent, and energy systems that power Michigan’s future

Michigan should not drift while other places invest in discovery, talent, and resilient infrastructure. Joshua Kent wants research and development treated as public strength: better science funding, stronger educational pathways, smarter technology policy, and cleaner, more reliable energy systems that support long-term prosperity.

Why this matters

Research and development is not a luxury item. It shapes jobs, public capacity, competitiveness, and whether a state is preparing for the next generation or merely reacting to it.

Joshua’s broader policy documents treat innovation as something that should serve the public, not just private extraction. That means supporting science, technology, data, energy, and education in ways that are transparent, durable, and tied to real opportunity for ordinary people. This is a working agenda that will keep growing with research, expert input, and community feedback, but the direction is clear: invest early, build intelligently, and make the gains broad-based.

The three-part R&D agenda

Discovery, people, and infrastructure have to move together.

Back science and public research

Michigan should strengthen research partnerships with universities, labs, technical programs, and mission-driven public institutions. Joshua wants public policy to be more evidence-based, more scientifically serious, and more willing to invest in long-term discovery.

Build stronger education-to-work pipelines

The state needs more than slogans about opportunity. Joshua’s platform supports stronger science and systems literacy, broader pathways through community college, trade and apprenticeship programs, and adult reskilling so talent is not wasted by cost or geography.

Modernize energy as public resilience

Energy, water, and infrastructure should be treated as resilience systems. Joshua supports cleaner power, stronger grid capacity, distributed energy, storage, and upgrades that improve reliability instead of leaving communities exposed to fragile systems.

What Joshua would push for

A serious public-investment strategy should connect schools, research institutions, infrastructure, and local industry.

Support Michigan research ecosystems

Public leaders should help strengthen the research environments that already exist across the state, including partnerships that connect discovery with practical problem-solving in health, energy, manufacturing, agriculture, and education.

Make science and technical literacy part of opportunity

Joshua’s education framework calls for stronger science, media, data, civic, and financial literacy so students are better prepared for modern life and a changing economy. He also supports equal-status pathways into trades, certifications, community college, and apprenticeships.

Accelerate clean and reliable energy investment

The goal is not just “green branding.” It is real public resilience: stronger grid modernization, microgrids for critical services, storage, cleaner generation, and infrastructure built for life-cycle value rather than the cheapest short-term fix.

Use technology with public guardrails

Emerging technology should help people, not deepen surveillance, bias, or concentrated control. Joshua wants data and AI policy approached with transparency, accountability, and a clear public-interest standard.

What Michigan could gain

Smart investment in research and development can make life more affordable, more skilled, and more resilient.

Better jobs and stronger local economies

When innovation is paired with local training and public investment, more of the upside stays in communities instead of being extracted elsewhere.

Cleaner, steadier infrastructure

Better energy systems and smarter long-life infrastructure mean fewer failures, stronger public capacity, and lower long-term waste.

A state that prepares instead of scrambling

Joshua wants Michigan to become more future-ready by investing before crisis forces a rushed and expensive response.

Help build a smarter, stronger Michigan

This campaign is working toward a public-investment model that connects education, science, energy, and real-world opportunity.
