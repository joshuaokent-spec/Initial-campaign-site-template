For Educators & Students

Schools should build capability, dignity, and real opportunity

Joshua Kent’s time as a teacher and tutor made one thing clear: students do better when we expect more from them and support them more seriously. Schools should not be organized around fear, humiliation, rigid sorting, or one narrow definition of success. They should help young people grow into capable adults with multiple real paths forward.

What Joshua learned in the classroom

The problem is not a lack of student potential. Too often, it is a lack of humane systems and practical support.

Joshua’s broader education documents argue that opportunity must be built, not merely promised. In practice, that means schools that convert human potential into real capability, dignity, and mobility. It also means a system that stops confusing punishment with seriousness and starts building support structures worthy of students and educators.

What that looks like in policy

The campaign’s education agenda is guided by both reform and respect.

Protect student dignity and rights

Joshua supports moving away from corporal punishment and harmful zero-tolerance systems, while pushing back against discriminatory treatment and weak privacy protections for student data.

Replace narrow sorting with real support

His policy framework emphasizes tutoring, counseling, more balanced assessment, and stronger school supports so students are not written off because they need time, guidance, or a different route to show their ability.

Build equal-status pathways

Joshua supports a system where apprenticeships, trades, community college, certifications, entrepreneurship, and four-year college are all real options rather than a hierarchy where only one route is treated as success.

The kind of school system Joshua wants to build

Students should leave school more prepared for life, work, citizenship, and self-direction.

Teach more than test-taking

Joshua’s documents call for stronger literacy, science, civics, media, data, and financial literacy, with more project-based and capability-based learning instead of overreliance on narrow assessment models.

Start pathways earlier without locking kids in

He supports earlier exposure to trades, coding, arts, civics, and career exploration, while keeping bridge courses, counseling, and equity safeguards so specialization does not become permanent tracking.

Make advanced opportunity real everywhere

Rural students and students in under-resourced communities should not be locked out of apprenticeships, certifications, dual enrollment, tutoring, transportation, or device access because of ZIP code or family income.

Back educators with real development

Better teacher preparation, professional development, and partnerships with universities, cultural institutions, and industry should be part of the work of school improvement, not an afterthought.

Why this matters for Michigan

The state’s future depends on whether young people can grow into stable, skilled, and self-directed adults.

Stronger schools mean stronger communities

When students have dignity, educators have support, and families see real pathways ahead, communities become more stable and more hopeful.

Opportunity should not depend on luck

Joshua wants public education to make development more universal and more auditable, so access to growth is not quietly rationed by class or geography.

Michigan needs a broader definition of success

A healthy education system should prepare students for college, work, service, entrepreneurship, and community life instead of treating one narrow lane as the only valid future.

Help build schools around dignity and opportunity

Joshua Kent wants educators, students, and families to help shape a public education agenda that is serious, humane, and practical.
