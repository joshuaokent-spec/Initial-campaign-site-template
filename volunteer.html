Join the Team

Government for People, Not Power. Practical Reform for Public Good. Campaigns are built by people willing to show up, talk to their neighbors, and help turn ideas into public action. Volunteers make it possible to expand outreach, organize events, build trust, and carry a people-first reform message into real communities.

Ways to Get Involved

Whether you have a few hours a week or can only help occasionally, there is a place for you in this campaign.

Canvassing

Speak directly with voters in neighborhoods, share campaign information, and help build stronger local connections around reform, accountability, and people-first government.

Phone Banking

Reach supporters and community members by phone to share updates, answer questions, and encourage participation in events, outreach, and public discussion.

Event Support

Help with setup, greeting guests, organizing materials, and making campaign events welcoming, thoughtful, and well-run for everyone who attends.

Community Outreach

Help spread campaign information in your area, connect with local groups, and support direct engagement with residents who want a more accountable and reform-oriented politics.

Policy & Research Support

Assist with organizing ideas, feedback, and campaign materials related to democratic reform, labor policy, housing, education, and public systems.

Host or Help with a Gathering

Help create space for conversations in neighborhoods, community centers, or small public settings where people can ask questions and engage with the campaign directly.

Why volunteer?

Volunteering is one of the most meaningful ways to support a campaign. It helps build momentum, strengthens outreach, and creates more opportunities for real public conversation. This campaign is not built around spectacle alone. It is built around the slower, harder work of organizing, listening, and helping people believe public life can still be rebuilt around dignity, rights, and accountability.

Why this campaign needs people, not just donors

Money can help a campaign communicate, but people are what make it real. Volunteers bring the campaign into neighborhoods, conversations, public events, and communities that would otherwise only hear polished messaging from a distance. A people-first campaign should be built by actual people who care enough to take part in it.

Volunteer Sign-Up

Fill out the form below to let the campaign know how you would like to help.

What happens next?

After signing up, a member of the campaign team can follow up with more information about volunteer needs, event opportunities, and the best next steps based on your interests and availability. The goal is to help people plug into meaningful work, not just collect names.

Who should volunteer?

You do not need prior campaign experience to help. If you care about democratic reform, labor dignity, anti-corruption, education, housing, public systems, or simply want to support a more serious kind of public politics, there is room for you here.

Other ways to support

Not ready to volunteer yet? You can still support the campaign by attending an event, sharing campaign information with others, staying informed, or making a contribution to help expand outreach efforts.
