On the Issues

Government for People, Not Power. Practical Reform for Public Good. This campaign is built on the belief that public office should protect rights, resist corruption, raise the floor beneath ordinary people, and rebuild public systems so they actually serve human dignity instead of concentrated wealth, procedural gamesmanship, and institutional decay.

A people-first reform platform

This campaign is not only about reacting to problems. It is about redesigning the rules, incentives, and institutions that shape everyday life.

The core principle

Rights should be non-negotiable. Public power should be constrained by transparency, due process, and real accountability. Economic life should reward work, skill, innovation, and contribution, but never in ways that justify deprivation below a dignified social floor. Public systems should be durable, transparent, and built for long-term flourishing rather than short-term extraction or privatized decay.

Democratic Reform & Representation

Democracy is legitimate only when public consent is real, representation is meaningful, and institutions are not quietly distorted by money, party machinery, or gerrymandered maps. My platform supports a more direct, representative, and accountable democratic structure.

  • Fight partisan gerrymandering and support independent, transparent districting.
  • Protect broad voter access and stronger election legitimacy standards.
  • Support reforms that make representation more responsive and less distorted.
  • Reduce the power of procedural manipulation and political gatekeeping.
  • Push public institutions toward clearer accountability to actual voters.

Anti-Corruption & Public Accountability

Too much government assumes good faith from people with strong incentives to abuse power. I begin from a different premise: systems should be designed assuming corruption is possible, and structured so corruption is difficult to perform, easy to detect, and costly to attempt.

My policy framework includes stricter conflict-of-interest rules, stronger inspector and auditor systems, independent integrity enforcement, revolving-door restrictions, donor transparency, whistleblower protections, and real penalties for corruption and retaliation :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

  • Strengthen conflict-of-interest and financial disclosure rules.
  • Support independent auditing, oversight, and anti-capture enforcement.
  • Crack down on bribery, procurement fraud, dark money laundering, and self-dealing.
  • Protect whistleblowers and punish retaliation against them.
  • Treat serious corruption as a prison-level public offense, not a minor fine.

Workers, Wages, & Economic Justice

An economy should serve the people living in it. Work should provide dignity, stability, and a path to a better life. That means stronger labor power, a higher floor for ordinary people, and an economy that does not allow monopoly structures and corporate concentration to dominate wages, politics, and local opportunity.

My broader economic framework supports union strength, sector-based wage standards, anti-monopoly rules, public options where markets fail, progressive taxation, and stronger protections against wage theft, misclassification, and predatory labor arrangements :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6} :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7} :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}

  • Strengthen unions and worker bargaining power.
  • Support sector wage standards that reflect skill, safety, and real labor value.
  • Fight wage theft, misclassification, and exploitative gig-economy loopholes.
  • Encourage cooperative ownership, local rootedness, and small business development.
  • Constrain monopoly and oligopoly power that distorts labor and politics.

Public Banking, Finance, & Economic Stability

Money and finance should be treated as public infrastructure, not merely private extraction tools. My broader platform supports public banking, safer payments systems, tighter limits on systemically dominant banks, consumer protection, and financial structures that serve public development instead of speculative capture.

The longer-term framework includes postal banking, public banks at multiple levels, hard limits on “too big to fail,” structural separation of safe banking from risk banking, stronger anti-predation finance rules, and a public-development approach to capital and stability :contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}

  • Support public and community-centered banking options.
  • Reduce predatory lending, junk-fee extraction, and exploitative finance.
  • Push back against dangerous banking concentration and bailout dependence.
  • Strengthen consumer protection and financial transparency.
  • Encourage finance that supports productive communities rather than speculation alone.

Housing & Cost of Living

Housing is an essential need, not just an asset class for speculation. Families should not be pushed into instability because corporate actors, vacancy games, and artificial scarcity have turned shelter into a pressure point.

My larger housing platform includes a guaranteed baseline housing option, mixed public and regulated-affiliate models, anti-speculation tools, stronger tenant protections, vacancy and non-primary residence pressure, first-time ownership pathways, and public financing for housing expansion and repair :contentReference[oaicite:10]{index=10}

  • Support baseline affordable housing and stronger tenant protections.
  • Encourage practical zoning and supply reforms that expand real housing access.
  • Discourage speculative vacancy and concentrated investor ownership where it harms communities.
  • Support first-time ownership, co-op pathways, and community-rooted housing policy.
  • Link housing reform to infrastructure, transit, and neighborhood stability.

Education, Childcare, & Social Mobility

Education should build capability, confidence, and opportunity. It should not be organized around fear, narrow testing logic, or punitive systems that sort young people into success and failure before they have a fair chance to grow.

My education framework is much broader than generic school reform. It includes stronger protections for students, reduced dependence on harmful punitive discipline, reforms to standardized testing, more balanced assessment models, universal childcare and tutoring, stronger trade and apprenticeship pathways, and redesigned school structures built around capability and life-readiness :contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11} :contentReference[oaicite:12]{index=12} :contentReference[oaicite:13]{index=13}

  • Ban corporal punishment and move away from harmful zero-tolerance discipline.
  • Protect students from discriminatory policies, including anti-LGBTQ+ treatment and discriminatory dress codes.
  • Reduce overreliance on standardized testing and broaden forms of assessment.
  • Strengthen trades, apprenticeships, certifications, and postsecondary access.
  • Support universal childcare and tutoring as part of a serious equal-opportunity system.
  • Expand media, civic, financial, and real-life literacy in public education.

Healthcare, Mental Health, & Care Systems

A society cannot claim to value freedom while people are forced to navigate illness, disability, mental health crises, or caregiving burdens without stable support. Public systems should help people live, recover, and participate fully in community life.

My documents treat healthcare, mental healthcare, emergency capacity, and care systems as core public infrastructure, not optional burdens. That means stronger access, public continuity, crisis capacity, and a more serious care floor for ordinary people :contentReference[oaicite:14]{index=14} :contentReference[oaicite:15]{index=15}

  • Support broader healthcare and mental healthcare access.
  • Strengthen emergency care continuity and public response capacity.
  • Recognize caregiving and family support as core public concerns.
  • Improve access for disability, neurodiversity, and long-term support needs.
  • Treat health systems as public resilience assets, not optional services.

Justice, Public Safety, & Civil Rights

Public safety should be built on trust, transparency, due process, and the right professionals responding to the right crises. A justice system that criminalizes poverty, escalates nonviolent crises, and protects abuse is not truly keeping people safe.

My justice framework supports strong due process protections, better access to counsel, separate social response systems for many nonviolent crises, stronger police accountability, reforms to pretrial detention, rehabilitation-centered corrections, and a public-safety model built around trust and outcomes rather than fear alone :contentReference[oaicite:16]{index=16}

  • Protect due process, evidence transparency, and fair access to counsel.
  • Support separate social response systems for mental health, addiction, and nonviolent crises.
  • Strengthen police accountability, independent review, and anti-retaliation rules.
  • Reduce profit-driven detention and support rehabilitation-centered corrections reform.
  • Move away from poverty-based punishment and coercive pretrial systems.

Infrastructure, Energy, & Environmental Renewal

Public works should be durable, future-facing, and built for resilience. Infrastructure should not be something neglected until collapse, nor should energy and utilities be organized around extractive monopolies and short-term gains.

My larger framework supports durable infrastructure, public utility backbones, grid modernization, cleaner and more reliable energy mixes, circular-economy standards, restoration programs, and public investment designed for long-term resilience rather than perpetual breakdown and rebuild cycles :contentReference[oaicite:17]{index=17}

  • Modernize energy, water, transport, and communications as long-term public assets.
  • Support cleaner energy, stronger grid resilience, and public utility accountability.
  • Encourage waste reduction, recapture, and circular-economy policy.
  • Expand restoration and public works programs that strengthen both communities and ecosystems.
  • Prioritize durable, long-life infrastructure over lowest-bid short-termism.

Transparent Government & Institutional Reform

Good leadership requires more than promises. It requires public institutions that are understandable, reviewable, and worthy of trust. Government should not feel distant, obscure, or structurally unreadable to the people it governs.

My earlier reform drafts also emphasize constitutional governance, separation of powers, strengthened checks and balances, more public participation, and institutions built around civic accountability rather than elite insulation :contentReference[oaicite:18]{index=18} :contentReference[oaicite:19]{index=19}

  • Promote clearer, more understandable public decision-making.
  • Support stronger checks and balances and better public oversight mechanisms.
  • Reduce capture-by-obscurity and procedural black-box governance.
  • Encourage civic participation and more meaningful public review.
  • Make institutions more accountable to the people living under them.

A Michigan starting point, a broader reform vision

I am running for the Michigan House of Representatives because reform has to begin somewhere real. Michigan can help lead by proving that stronger democracy, better labor standards, broader educational opportunity, public accountability, and durable public systems are still possible.

This campaign is not built around empty branding or symbolic frustration. It is built around the conviction that public life can be rebuilt: with better rules, stronger rights, honest institutions, and a clearer commitment to ordinary people over concentrated private power.
