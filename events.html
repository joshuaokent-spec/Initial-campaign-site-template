Community Listening Sessions
Small-group conversations focused on housing, education, cost of living, and public trust.

These sessions are designed for residents who want a serious discussion instead of a rally-stage soundbite. The goal is to hear directly from people about what feels broken, what deserves protection, and what reform should look like in practice.

Volunteer Orientations
Hands-on briefings for supporters who want to help with outreach, events, research, and organizing.

Volunteer gatherings focus on how to represent the campaign well in public, where help is most needed, and how to turn interest into useful, people-first work.

Neighborhood Stops and Meetups
Smaller public visits with room for questions, follow-up, and direct conversation.

Not every useful event needs a stage. Some of the most valuable stops are the ones where people can speak candidly, ask difficult questions, and feel like the campaign is showing up to listen as much as it is showing up to persuade.

Bring a Friend

Public events are one of the easiest ways to introduce new people to the campaign and build credibility through real conversation.

Ask Better Questions

The goal is not to stage-manage agreement. Strong public events should make room for challenge, clarification, and honest disagreement.

Stay in the Loop

The fastest way to hear about confirmed dates is to sign up for updates or send a note through the contact page.

Why events matter

A reform campaign should not hide behind polished messaging alone. Public events matter because they create space for accountability, disagreement, and democratic participation. They help show whether a campaign is serious about people or only serious about image.
