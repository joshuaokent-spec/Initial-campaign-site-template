Upcoming Events

Government for People, Not Power. Practical Reform for Public Good. Campaign events are where ideas meet the public. Join Joshua Kent for town halls, volunteer gatherings, meet-and-greets, and other events focused on reform, accountability, and the everyday issues shaping life in Michigan.

Join the Campaign in Person

Real reform should be public, accessible, and rooted in conversation. These events are designed to give residents a chance to ask questions, share concerns, and help shape a people-first campaign.

What to expect at campaign events

Some events focus on public listening and open discussion, while others are centered on organizing, volunteer training, outreach planning, and direct engagement with communities across Michigan. Whether you are attending for the first time or coming back to help build momentum, you are welcome.

  • Community Town Hall with Joshua Kent
    Saturday, February 21 • 2:00 PM • Downtown Community Center

    Join Joshua Kent for a public town hall focused on democratic reform, corruption, worker dignity, cost of living, and the larger question of what government should owe to ordinary people. Residents are invited to ask questions, raise concerns, and hear directly from the campaign.

  • Volunteer Kickoff Meeting
    Thursday, February 26 • 6:00 PM • Campaign Field Office

    New and returning volunteers are invited to take part in campaign orientation, learn more about outreach priorities, and find concrete ways to help support public engagement, event preparation, voter contact, and movement-building around reform.

  • Neighborhood Meet and Greet
    Tuesday, March 3 • 5:30 PM • Westside Park Pavilion

    Stop by for an informal evening conversation with Joshua Kent and the campaign. This is a chance to meet in a more relaxed environment, ask questions about the platform, and discuss the challenges and priorities facing local communities.

Bring a Friend

Campaign events are one of the best ways to introduce others to the mission and expand a politics rooted in public accountability, dignity, and real structural reform.

Volunteer Opportunities

Many events include sign-up opportunities for canvassing, outreach, phone banking, organizing support, and future event participation.

Stay Updated

Check back regularly as more appearances, public discussions, and campaign events are added throughout the cycle.

Why events matter

A campaign built on reform should not be hidden behind polished messaging alone. Public events matter because they create space for challenge, disagreement, clarification, and democratic participation. They are one of the clearest ways a campaign can demonstrate whether it is serious about accountability or only serious about image.
