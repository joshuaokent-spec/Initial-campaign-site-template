Why this campaign matters

Michigan can help prove that democratic reform, worker dignity, and accountable public service are still possible.

This campaign is rooted in a broader vision of political and economic reform: stronger democratic representation, real anti-corruption safeguards, protections against concentrated corporate power, a higher floor for working people, and public institutions designed to actually serve the common good. The goal is not simply to win office, but to help build a model of government that works for people again.

What Joshua Kent stands for

A reform agenda built around rights, accountability, public dignity, and institutions designed to resist corruption by structure rather than by hope.

Joshua Kent’s platform begins from a simple idea: government should be built to serve people, not powerful interests. That means rights must remain non-negotiable, public power must be constrained by transparency and oversight, and economic life must be organized so that ordinary people can actually build stable lives instead of being crushed by extraction, concentration, and institutional decay.

Assume Corruption, Build Guardrails

Public institutions should be designed around the assumption that concentrated power invites abuse. That means stronger anti-corruption architecture, conflict-of-interest rules, enforcement mechanisms, and clear public transparency instead of symbolic ethics language alone.

Raise the Floor, Protect Rights

A decent society should guarantee a strong baseline: access to education, healthcare, housing, and fair work. Merit, effort, and innovation should matter, but they should never be used to justify deprivation below a dignified floor.

Break Capture by Wealth

Democracy cannot function well when political influence, labor markets, and essential systems are dominated by monopoly power and corporate consolidation. Markets should remain free where healthy, but tightly constrained where they become abusive or structurally anti-democratic.

A practical reform platform

The campaign is grounded in concrete reform areas, not just slogans.

Democracy & Representation

Support reforms that reduce wasted votes, fight gerrymandering, improve public access to voting, and make government more responsive to the people who live under it.

Anti-Corruption & Public Integrity

Crack down on bribery, revolving-door abuse, procurement fraud, hidden donor influence, and retaliation against watchdogs or whistleblowers.

Labor, Wages, & Ownership

Strengthen unions, protect workers from wage theft and misclassification, encourage sector standards, and support local ownership, small business, and cooperative development.

Housing & Cost of Living

Treat housing as a human necessity, not just a speculative commodity. Expand baseline affordability, reduce predatory rent pressure, and support first-time ownership pathways.

Education & Social Mobility

Reform punitive school practices, broaden educational pathways, strengthen childcare and tutoring, and build a system that develops capability instead of sorting people into failure.

Public Systems & Infrastructure

Build durable infrastructure, strengthen public care systems, modernize energy and resilience planning, and invest in institutions meant to last rather than systems built to decay and be privatized later.

Campaign Priorities

A people-first platform grounded in reform, dignity, and public accountability.

Why start in Michigan House?

Big reform ideas need a place to begin in practice.

Joshua Kent is running for the Michigan House of Representatives because reform has to become real in people’s lives. Michigan is a place where democratic accountability, labor dignity, public investment, educational reform, and anti-corruption safeguards can be advanced in concrete ways. State-level leadership can help prove that better systems are possible — and that public life does not have to keep drifting toward concentrated private power, corporate libertarianism, and late-stage institutional decline.

This campaign is the beginning of a broader effort to rethink how government works, who it serves, and what ordinary people should be able to expect from public institutions. The aim is not simply to react to crisis after crisis, but to help build durable systems that reward contribution, protect liberty, raise the floor for everyone, and restore public faith in democratic government.
