For Small Business Owners

Main Street businesses deserve a fair shot to compete and grow

Small business owners are too often squeezed from both sides: by concentrated corporate power above them and by unstable financing, workforce pressure, and administrative strain below them. Joshua Kent wants a policy agenda that makes room for local ownership, fairer competition, and more practical access to the tools businesses need to survive.

What is broken now

Michigan’s local businesses cannot thrive if the rules keep tilting toward concentrated wealth and market capture.

Consolidation distorts the market

Joshua’s broader economic framework is anti-concentration by design. When giant firms dominate labor markets, supply chains, or political access, small businesses lose room to compete on fair terms.

Access to capital is too uneven

Local entrepreneurs often face a financing landscape built around scale, connections, or extraction. Joshua wants stronger community finance, public banking tools, and local lending pathways that are easier to reach and harder to game.

The talent pipeline is too disconnected

Small businesses need more than generic workforce talk. They need schools, training programs, and public institutions that actually help develop workers with usable skills and pathways into local opportunity.

What Joshua would fight for

Small business policy should be about fair conditions, not just ceremonial praise.

Fairer competition through anti-monopoly reform

Joshua supports stronger guardrails against concentration and market capture so local businesses are not constantly outmatched by firms large enough to dominate labor, logistics, or politics.

More local and public-backed finance

His platform includes stronger public, community, co-op, and credit-union-style finance options. The goal is not to crowd out healthy enterprise, but to make productive local investment more available and less extractive.

Better entrepreneurship and ownership pathways

Joshua’s education framework explicitly supports entrepreneurship, co-op literacy, small business operations, bookkeeping, compliance knowledge, and incubator-style capstones so ownership is treated as a real pathway, not a fringe idea.

Stronger local training partnerships

The state should do more to connect schools, apprenticeships, community colleges, and local employers so small businesses are not left trying to solve workforce development entirely on their own.

Why this matters beyond business owners alone

Local ownership affects wages, neighborhood stability, community character, and whether wealth circulates or disappears upward.

  • Healthier small business conditions help communities keep more talent, more ownership, and more economic resilience close to home.
  • Joshua wants a system where innovation, work, and ownership are rewarded without allowing monopoly power to quietly rewrite the rules.
  • The long-term direction is an economy with a higher floor, more room for entrepreneurship, and stronger community-rooted institutions.

Bring local business experience into the campaign

Joshua Kent wants to hear from business owners who know firsthand where policy helps, where it hinders, and where reform is overdue.
