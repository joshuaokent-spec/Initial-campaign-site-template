About Joshua

A life shaped by family, service, education, and second chances

Joshua Kent is a family man whose views were shaped by real struggle, not just political theory. He knows what it feels like when college does not go as planned, what service asks of a person, what it means to go back and rebuild after a difficult chapter, and how much potential young people carry when someone finally meets them with patience, standards, and support.

The path that shaped this campaign

Joshua’s biography is not a perfect straight line. It is a series of lived experiences that made him more serious about dignity, opportunity, and structural reform.

Family comes first

Joshua’s public life begins with private responsibility. Being family-centered changes how you see policy: stability matters more, affordability matters more, and public systems stop feeling abstract. Housing, schools, wages, healthcare, and public trust all look different when you are thinking about the people who depend on you.

College did not come easily the first time

Joshua knows what it means to struggle in college and to feel the gap between talent and stability. That experience shaped his belief that our systems are too quick to sort people into “success” and “failure” without giving them enough structure, support, or time to grow into what they are capable of becoming.

Military service added discipline and perspective

Time in the military reinforced the importance of duty, accountability, and service larger than oneself. It also deepened Joshua’s sense that institutions work best when they are clear in purpose, serious about standards, and organized to support the people inside them rather than simply consume them.

He went back, finished the work, and entered the classroom

After leaving the military, Joshua returned to college with a sharper sense of purpose. Later, as a teacher and tutor, he saw how much intelligence, creativity, and drive students carry, and how often broken systems get in their way. That is where the belief solidified: we do not just need better messaging. We need better systems.

What those experiences taught him

The campaign message “Government for People, Not Power” comes from lived experience.

People need a real floor beneath them

Joshua believes rights should be non-negotiable and that ordinary people should have a dignified baseline: real access to education, healthcare, housing, and fair work. Merit matters, but it should never be used to justify deprivation.

Systems should offer second chances, not just penalties

Struggle in school, work, or family life should not become a permanent sentence. Public policy should help people recover, regroup, and move forward instead of trapping them in failure after one hard chapter.

Power needs guardrails

Joshua’s broader reform framework assumes corruption is always possible. That means stronger transparency, tighter ethics rules, clearer public records, and institutions built to resist capture rather than simply hope leaders behave well.

Why start in the Michigan House?

Big ideas matter most when they become visible in people’s daily lives.

  • Joshua is running for the Michigan House because state government is close enough to daily life for reform to be felt in schools, housing, wages, public transparency, and infrastructure.
  • He wants to prove that people-first reform can work in practice, not just in theory, by fighting for cleaner government, stronger public investment, and more dignified institutions.
  • The long-term goal is larger than one office: build a serious record of public service in Lansing and carry that same reform logic into broader statewide leadership and, eventually, future Senate work.
