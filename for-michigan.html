For Michigan

Government for People, Not Power. Practical Reform for Public Good. The same priorities I would fight for on behalf of Lansing can also help strengthen Michigan as a whole: cleaner government, more stable housing, and schools built around dignity and opportunity. My goal is to help make this state a place of pride for workers, families, students, and communities — not a place where ordinary people are asked to live with instability while power serves itself.

What is good for Lansing can help lead Michigan

State-level reform should not remain trapped in theory. It should be visible in daily life across urban, suburban, and rural Michigan.

The reforms I want to fight for are not just local slogans. They reflect a broader vision of public life: stronger democratic accountability, real anti-corruption safeguards, better conditions for workers and families, more stable housing, more humane schools, and a government designed to support dignity instead of managing decline. If these reforms can help improve life in Lansing, they can also help strengthen Michigan as a whole.

The statewide test of reform

A serious public agenda should be able to answer simple questions people ask everywhere in the state: Will this make my life more stable? Will this make government more honest? Will this help my family, my community, my work, and my future feel less fragile? These are the questions I want policy to answer clearly.

Priority 1: Clean Up State Power

Michigan deserves a government that people can trust, understand, and believe is actually answerable to them.

What I would fight for

I would fight for stronger ethics laws, more visible lobbying and contracting records, tighter conflict-of-interest rules, stronger whistleblower protections, and public systems that make abuse of power harder to hide.

My broader anti-corruption framework is built around the principle that systems should assume corruption is possible and be designed so abuse is harder to perform, easier to detect, and more costly to attempt :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

What this means for Michigan

A cleaner, more accountable state government benefits every region of Michigan, not just the capital. When the rules are clearer and the system is less captured, people across the state have more reason to trust public institutions.

What people could actually feel

More confidence that decisions are not quietly being shaped by insiders alone. More visibility into how public money, influence, and access are being used.

Why it matters

Public trust is not rebuilt through slogans. It is rebuilt when government is visibly more accountable, less corrupt, and less shielded from the people living under it.

In plain terms

People across Michigan should be able to believe that the state belongs to the public, not to whoever is best positioned to influence it from behind closed doors.

Priority 2: Make Housing More Stable

Housing security should not depend on how useful you are to speculation.

What I would fight for

I would fight for stronger housing stability measures, better tools for expanding practical housing access, pressure against harmful speculation and vacancy where appropriate, stronger renter protections, and better pathways into long-term housing security and ownership.

My larger housing framework treats housing as an essential need and includes stronger baseline delivery systems, anti-hoarding and anti-vacancy pressure, and more rooted paths into housing security :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

What this means for Michigan

Whether in cities, small towns, or rural communities, housing instability harms family life, local growth, neighborhood health, and long-term security. A more stable housing framework helps communities stay rooted.

What people could actually feel

Less fear that rent, neglect, displacement, or speculative pressure will destabilize their home life. More ability to imagine staying where they live and building something lasting there.

Why it matters

Housing affects everything else: family finances, schools, mental health, work, community continuity, and whether people feel they belong in the future of the place they live.

In plain terms

A home should feel like the foundation beneath a person’s life — not the thing most likely to collapse under them first.

Priority 3: Build Schools Around Dignity and Opportunity

Schools should help build futures, not normalize punishment, exclusion, and narrow measures of worth.

What I would fight for

I would fight for school reform that reduces harmful punitive discipline, strengthens student dignity, expands tutoring and support systems, and broadens practical educational pathways through apprenticeships, certifications, and more life-ready structures.

My education documents already support stronger protections against corporal punishment, zero-tolerance systems, discriminatory treatment, and weak one-size-fits-all models while expanding more balanced, humane, and capable forms of educational development :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2} :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

What this means for Michigan

Families across Michigan deserve school systems that prepare students for adulthood with dignity, support, skill, and real opportunity instead of excessive punishment or narrow sorting.

What people could actually feel

More confidence that schools are trying to build futures. Better support for struggling students. More respect for trade, certification, apprenticeship, and multiple pathways into meaningful adult life.

Why it matters

The future of Michigan depends on whether young people leave school with confidence, skill, and real options — not just whether they survived a system that treated them like compliance problems.

In plain terms

Schools should make young people feel more capable, not more disposable.

What these 3 priorities say about Michigan

Clean government, stable homes, and better futures for young people are not side issues. They are part of rebuilding the moral center of public life.

For workers

A stronger, more honest state should work for the people whose labor keeps it running — not just for those best positioned to extract from it. My broader ideology is explicitly worker-centered, pro-labor, anti-concentration, and committed to raising the floor beneath ordinary people :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

For industry

Michigan should be a state known for productive strength, durable infrastructure, skilled labor, and systems designed for real development — not just financial games, institutional drift, or captured public life.

For families

Families should be able to imagine a future in Michigan with more stability, better schools, more honest institutions, and a stronger sense that public life exists to support them rather than abandon them.

After serving Lansing in the Michigan House

Public service should build on itself. If I serve well in the Michigan House, I want that work to continue at the national level.

A future goal: representing Michigan in the U.S. Senate

My aim after serving Lansing well in the Michigan House is to pursue a future role in the United States Senate, representing Michigan as a whole. I do not see that as a break from this work. I see it as a continuation of it.

I served my country in the United States Army, and I want my public career to reflect that same ethic of service: duty to something larger than myself, loyalty to the people I represent, and a willingness to fight for the dignity, strength, and long-term wellbeing of the communities I serve.

What that larger mission means

My ideology is not built around serving the corporate elite or protecting the comfort of captured systems. It is built around fighting for workers, industry, and families — for a Michigan that can become a point of pride again, and for a country that treats its people as worth investing in. I want both this state and this nation to be places people can point to with pride because they are more just, more capable, more stable, and more serious about the lives of real human beings.

How I would carry that vision forward

Service in Lansing should demonstrate what kind of public servant I am before I ask to represent the whole state.

Prove it in office first

My first responsibility is to show, in the Michigan House, that I can push real reforms, communicate honestly, and stay rooted in the needs of the people I represent.

Represent Michigan with pride

If I later seek the Senate, it will be to represent Michigan as a whole with the same seriousness: fighting for workers, families, industry, and public dignity against systems built for elite comfort.

Keep public service grounded

The goal is not office for its own sake. The goal is to keep pushing for structural reform in places where it can actually change lives.

What I want Michigan to become

I want Michigan to be a state that respects workers, builds durable industry, protects families, educates young people with dignity, governs honestly, and makes people feel that they live in a place worth believing in. I want it to be a point of pride for the people who live here — not because it is perfect, but because it is trying seriously to serve them.

This is about real people, not elite comfort

My politics are grounded in the belief that the lives of ordinary people should matter more than the preferences of the wealthy, the insulated, and the captured institutions that too often dominate public life. If I am given the chance to serve, I want to use that service to help reform the lives of real people — not to make the corporate elite more comfortable while everyone else is told to endure decline with patience.
