For the People

Rights should be non-negotiable, and government should answer to the public

Joshua Kent’s campaign starts with a simple belief: government should be built for ordinary people, not captured by wealth, corruption, or procedural games. Public power should protect liberty, raise the floor beneath working families, and make abuse of power harder to hide and harder to sustain.

Rights Civil, political, and social guarantees should be taken seriously.
Trust Government earns legitimacy through transparency and accountability.
Dignity People need a real floor beneath them, not rhetoric alone.

The core philosophy

Joshua’s policy charter centers liberty, equity, solidarity, progress, efficiency, and merit in that order.

  • Rights are non-negotiable
  • Raise the floor beneath ordinary people
  • Reward contribution above that floor
  • Assume corruption and build guardrails
  • Invest in durable public systems
  • Protect rural-urban cohesion

Joshua believes a decent society should guarantee a strong baseline of education, healthcare, housing, mental care, and fair access to work. Innovation, effort, and contribution matter, but they should never be used to justify life beneath a dignified floor.

What that means in practice

A people-first agenda is not vague compassion. It is structural design.

Clean government

Public institutions should assume corruption is possible and be structured to resist it through ethics rules, transparency, audits, anti-retaliation protections, and enforcement strong enough to matter.

Real guarantees people can feel

Joshua wants public policy organized around whether people can actually build stable lives: learn, work, raise families, stay healthy, and remain housed without being quietly crushed by broken systems.

Durable public systems

Infrastructure, education, energy, and care systems should be designed for long-term use and accountability, not left to decay until crisis creates another excuse for privatized failure.

Why the campaign says “Government for People, Not Power”

Because concentrated power keeps rewriting the rules unless ordinary people build stronger institutions.

People should not live at the mercy of extraction

Housing, healthcare, education, finance, and work should not be organized so ordinary people are endlessly pressured while the gains concentrate upward.

Representation should feel real

Democracy becomes fragile when it is distorted by money, insider networks, or procedural manipulation. Joshua wants public trust rebuilt through more transparent, more representative, more accountable governance.

Safety and prosperity should reinforce freedom

Joshua’s vision treats safety and prosperity as things that help people flourish with stronger communities, more practical freedom, and the ability to pursue work, education, family life, and civic life with less precarity.

This begins in Lansing and grows from there

Joshua is running for the Michigan House because reform has to start where people can feel it. Over time, he wants to help carry this same people-first logic into broader statewide leadership and, eventually, future Senate conversations.

Build a government that serves ordinary people again

This campaign is about more than one election. It is about proving that public life can still be honest, competent, and worthy of trust.
